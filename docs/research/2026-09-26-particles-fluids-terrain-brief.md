# Research brief: particles, fluids, and terrain for Elements Suite

**Date:** 2026-09-26

**Purpose:** A source-backed map of research bets and future workstreams. Paper methods are candidates for experiments, not implemented features or promises of commercial superiority.

## Executive direction

Elements Suite has a working GPU gas and fire path in Ember. The [core design](../superpowers/specs/2026-09-19-elements-suite-core-design.md) proposes Tide liquids, Strata terrain, and Weave procedural assets later; those are product directions, not delivered solvers. The strongest near-term strategy is to make Ember's physics and authoring measurably better, then reuse its graph, GPU, cache, and Blender seams for a particle/grid liquid solver and a layered erosion solver. Pursue learned detail or preview only after a classical result and evaluation set exist.

“Better” needs a target scene, quality threshold, hardware, and workflow. Houdini already offers sparse Pyro, FLIP with reseeding, whitewater, and multiscale erosion; JangaFX LiquiGen offers a sparse particle/voxel hybrid with meshing; Blender offers liquid secondary spray/foam/bubbles and Geometry Nodes simulation caching; Gaea offers artist-directed erosion; [GeoGen](https://jangafx.com/software/geogen) advertises node-based real-time terrain, erosion, particle/grid simulations, and game exports. [EmberGen 2 sparse simulation](https://jangafx.com/roadmap/embergen/future-features) is marked complete internally, with a Q4 2026 public beta target; it is not a public released baseline as of this brief. A broad claim of superiority is currently unsupported. [Houdini sparse Pyro](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/pyro/sparsity.html), [LiquiGen](https://docs.jangafx.com/liquigen/pages/getting_started.html), [FLIP](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/flipsolver.html), [whitewater](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/whitewatersolver.html), [erosion](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/heightfield_erode), [Blender simulation zones](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/modeling/geometry_nodes/simulation/simulation_zone.html), [Blender liquid particles](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.5/physics/fluid/type/domain/liquid/particles.html), [Gaea erosion](https://docs.quadspinner.com/Guide/Using-Gaea/Erosion.html).

## Current evidence and constraints

The repository's [current Mantaflow comparison](../bench/results.md) reports Ember fire at 64³/128³/256³ as 6.0/5.6/4.9 times faster in its measured bake setup, but with roughly 4–28 times the divergence and different flame shape/duration; the 128³ fire preview exceeds the 100 ms target under load. The report flags high host load across runs and explicitly calls for idle reruns. The current result is a speed/quality trade, not a win on all axes. The existing comparison is against Blender Mantaflow on one machine, not Houdini, LiquiGen, or Gaea, and cache/memory conventions must be matched before making vendor comparisons.

Implementation constraints from [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md): Rust engine and GPL Blender extension are separate; core must remain `Apache-2.0 OR MIT`, with no GPL source copied into crates; `wgpu` uses no optional features, scalar storage textures are R32Float, and shader stages have a four-storage-texture limit. ML is an optional seam, never needed for correct output. These constraints favor portable tiled buffers and explicit passes over assuming CUDA or a large bind set.

| Adaptive two-phase FLIP | [Adaptive Phase-Field-FLIP (SIGGRAPH 2025)](https://animation.rwth-aachen.de/publication/0594/) combines adaptive FLIP and phase-field methods for large two-phase scenes. | A later Tide target for liquid-air detail. | Fluid and granular coupling | [Adaptive GPU Kinetic Solver for Fluid-Granular Flows (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14982) couples adaptive lattice-Boltzmann fluid blocks with material-point sand and snow. | A later Tide–Strata interface experiment, after each material has an independently validated solver; its reported results do not establish portable `wgpu` performance. |
| Long-range fluid advection | [Eulerian-Lagrangian Fluid Simulation on Particle Flow Maps (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09672) proposes particle flow maps for accurate long-range advection; [Cirrus (SIGGRAPH 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3731190) adds adaptive hybrid particle/grid flow maps. | A gas-vorticity and Tide transport research branch. Compare against the current RK2/MacCormack implementation, including memory and boundary costs; flow maps are established research, not a novelty claim. |
| Learned flow representation | [Fluid Simulation on Neural Flow Maps (SIGGRAPH Asia 2023)](https://yitongdeng-projects.github.io/neural_flow_maps_webpage/) compresses flow-map representation. | Optional research path for fine detail or storage, with conservation and held-out geometry checks. |
| Learned fluid editing | [Fast Subspace Fluid Simulation with Temporal-Aware Basis (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05339) explores reduced temporal bases for interactive editing, upscaling, and turbulence. | Consider as an optional preview/detail layer after truth-based bake comparisons. Check out-of-distribution behavior and temporal coherence. |
| Infinite learned terrain | [Terrain Diffusion (2025 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08309) proposes seed-consistent, seamless random access to diffusion-generated terrain. | Later Strata candidate only: reproduce continuity, drainage, determinism, latency, and model-license checks against a procedural baseline. Preprint claims are not validated for this suite. |
| Multiscale terrain | [Terrain Amplification using Multi-scale Erosion (SIGGRAPH 2024)](https://research.adobe.com/publication/terrain-amplification-using-multi-scale-erosion/) combines thermal, stream-power, and hillslope approximations across scales. | Strong Strata reference: hydrologic structure before high-frequency texture. Compare drainage networks at several resolutions. |
| Steep slopes | [Efficient Debris-flow Simulation for Steep Terrain Erosion (SIGGRAPH 2024)](https://www-sop.inria.fr/reves/Basilic/2024/JBC24/) combines debris-flow and fluvial erosion with a unified GPU algorithm. | A route past uniformly softened ridges; needs material and sediment accounting. |
| Overhang-capable terrain | [3D Real-Time Hydraulic Erosion Simulation using Multi-Layered Heightmaps (VMV 2024)](https://diglib.eg.org/items/deefa865-6a25-4463-adcd-d8de2b37507e) extends heightmap erosion toward layered 3D structures. | Candidate after 2.5D terrain is reliable; ordinary heightmaps cannot express caves or overhangs. |

## Proposed workstreams

### E1 — Ember quality before more solver breadth

**Question:** Can Ember preserve its interaction advantage while bringing fire divergence, flame shape, and fuel burnout into a predeclared tolerance against Mantaflow? Profile pressure solve, boundary handling, advection, and fire coupling by stage; test one change at a time. Evaluate plume, wind, collider, and fire at 64³/128³/256³ with matched source, frame rate, cache policy, and final render. Gate on residual/divergence, scalar and fuel mass accounting, centroid, flame lifetime, image comparison, peak memory, and median/p95 frame time. The [current report](../bench/results.md) gives the baseline and cautions. Follow with a sparse/tiled gas experiment only if active volume is low enough to offset tile management; Houdini's [sparse Pyro documentation](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/pyro/sparsity.html) explains both its advantage and the vacuum/padding behavior that can alter results.

### P1 — Shared particle substrate

**Question:** Can a portable GPU particle store support Tide, secondary fire particles, and sediment without separate infrastructure? Prototype structure-of-arrays buffers, spatial binning, deterministic seeded emission, stable IDs, compaction, collision queries, attribute transfer, and cache/export. First demonstrate millions of simple advected particles with stable ordering and bounded memory on Metal and software Vulkan; then validate neighbor-search and conservation on small CPU reference scenes. Distinguish particles that carry mass from visual tracers. Blender's secondary particles and Houdini's [whitewater solver](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/whitewatersolver.html) are workflow baselines, not equivalent physics baselines.

### T1 — Tide classical liquid baseline

**Question:** Can particle/grid FLIP with explicit reseeding, pressure projection, and a signed-distance surface deliver useful water shots on the portable engine? Start with dam break, splash, moving obstacle, and thin-sheet scenes. Measure liquid volume loss, pressure residual, penetration, surface roughness, spray distribution, and bake cost; compare with Blender Mantaflow and Houdini FLIP only under matched scene and export settings. Houdini's [FLIP documentation](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/flipsolver.html) makes the particle/grid split and reseeding controls explicit. Add APIC or adaptive grids only when the baseline identifies angular-momentum loss or empty-domain cost. Reserve phase-field/two-phase methods for a later experiment; the [2025 adaptive paper](https://animation.rwth-aachen.de/publication/0594/) does not establish a drop-in portable implementation.

### S1 — Strata hydrologic terrain

**Question:** Can one material-aware, seeded terrain graph produce believable macro drainage and fine erosion without resolution-specific retuning? Begin with heightfield rain/runoff, sediment entrainment/deposition, thermal weathering, and drainage diagnostics. Compare watershed topology, channel distribution, sediment balance, seam continuity, authoring time, and GPU cost against Houdini HeightField Erode and Gaea on identical input heightmaps. Use [multiscale amplification](https://research.adobe.com/publication/terrain-amplification-using-multi-scale-erosion/) and [debris-flow erosion](https://www-sop.inria.fr/reves/Basilic/2024/JBC24/) as separate ablation candidates. Add layered heightmaps only for scenes whose overhangs justify the extra state and export complexity. Diffusion-based texture/detail should remain optional and conditioned on the solved drainage/material fields.

### C1 — Coupled effects and learned detail

**Question:** Which cross-domain effects make one suite more useful than isolated products? Test liquid-to-foam, water-to-sediment, fire-to-sparks/ash, and terrain-to-runoff as explicit typed graph edges with source provenance. A learned upscaler or surrogate may improve preview/detail, but require comparison to a higher-resolution classical reference on held-out geometries, seeds, and forcing; report temporal flicker, volume/mass error, and failures. Keep the model downloadable separately and allow a full classical bake without it, per the [core design](../superpowers/specs/2026-09-19-elements-suite-core-design.md).

### W1 — Weave simulation-derived assets

**Question:** Can artists turn the suite's simulated fields and particles into reusable, editable game assets without reconstructing them in another tool? Prototype typed exports from Ember, Tide, and Strata into tiled flow maps, masks, flipbooks, and packed texture channels, with frame and seed provenance. Measure seam error, temporal looping, export fidelity, GPU memory, and time from source simulation to accepted asset. [IlluGen already offers](https://jangafx.com/software/illugen) 2D/3D procedural graphs, flow maps, flipbooks, meshes, and VAT, so a basic asset graph alone is not a differentiator; test whether the shared simulation graph and Blender handoff improve the end-to-end task.

## Evaluation and priority

1. **Now:** E1 and a reusable benchmark harness. Fix known fire quality gaps and rerun idle, same-scene benchmarks before claiming an improvement.
2. **Next:** P1 and T1 on small scenes. A classical liquid solver provides the truth data needed for adaptive and learned experiments. Sparse grids and a hybrid particle/voxel solver are already commercial features in [LiquiGen](https://docs.jangafx.com/liquigen/pages/getting_started.html), while [GeoGen](https://jangafx.com/software/geogen) already targets real-time terrain authoring. Differentiation must come from measured quality, portability, control, integration, or cost.
3. **In parallel after graph/data interfaces settle:** S1 with 2.5D hydrologic and material invariants; defer layered 3D until a target scene requires it.
4. **Research bets:** sparse/tiled Ember, adaptive two-phase Tide, coupled effects, Weave asset handoff, and learned detail. Each needs an ablation against its simpler baseline and a clear memory/time/quality Pareto improvement.

For every workstream, retain fixtures, hardware/backend identifiers, source assets, seeds, tolerances, raw timing distributions, peak resident/GPU memory, and rendered comparisons. Validate on native Metal and Linux software Vulkan; neither alone proves the other. Define an artist task and measure setup-to-acceptable-result time alongside solver speed. Treat a paper's reported speedup as evidence for its own implementation and test conditions, never as a forecast for this codebase. Review paper and repository licenses before incorporating source or pretrained weights.
